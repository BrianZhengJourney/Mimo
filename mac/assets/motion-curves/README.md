# Motion curves

> Scope checked 2026-08-31: retained deterministic gait reference; it does not replace the current runtime or Starter Action contracts.

One walking cycle, stored as joint-angle curves rather than as pictures.

`side-walk-v1.json` is 3.5 KB and holds a complete left-plus-right gait. It is
generated by `mac/tools/extract_walk_motion_curves.py` from
`mac/assets/motion-driver/mimo-side-walk-v1`, and previewed by
`mac/tools/preview_walk_motion_curves.py`.

## Why this exists

Two earlier attempts each solved half the problem:

- **Wan-Animate** (`artifacts/wan/runs/mimo-side-walk-v1-p0`) produced convincing
  gait timing, but repainted the character every frame. Hair shifted from brown
  to red, shoes grew coloured patches, and the drawn line work dissolved.
- **The walk-rig prototype** (`mac/prototypes/walk-rig`) kept the drawn pixels
  perfectly, because they *are* the drawn pixels, but its limbs were placeholder
  rectangles and its timing was hand-authored sine waves.

This asset is the first half of joining them: it keeps Wan's motion source and
discards its pixels. The character's appearance never enters this file.

## How to read it

Every channel is a Fourier series over one gait period:

```
value(t) = mean + Σ_k  cos[k]·cos(2π(k+1)t) + sin[k]·sin(2π(k+1)t)
```

with `t` the phase in `[0, 1)`. There is no frame table, so a renderer can
evaluate any phase and run at the display's refresh rate instead of stepping
through baked frames. The series is periodic by construction, so the loop
closes exactly.

- **Angles** are absolute segment directions in degrees: `0` points straight
  down, positive rotates toward the facing direction. They are *not* relative
  to the parent joint.
- **Lengths** are multiples of the leg (hip to ankle).
- **The far side** of the body is the same curve at `t + 0.5`. Only the near
  side was measured.

### Applying a channel to a drawn limb

Rotate each layer by `channel(t) − mean`, not by `channel(t)`. Each channel's
`mean` is that segment's average pose across the cycle, which absorbs anatomy
that has nothing to do with walking — `foot` averages +57° because an ankle-to-
toe vector points forward, and `headLean` averages +39° because it is measured
shoulder-to-nose on a face seen side-on. A drawn limb already sits at its own
rest orientation, so it needs the deviation, not the absolute.

## What was measured and what was inferred

Only the camera-side limbs are measured. In a locked side view the far arm is
behind the torso, and the estimator says so plainly: mean visibility for the
far elbow was **0.042** against **0.991** for the near one. Rather than trust
those guesses, the far side is synthesised by shifting the near side half a
period.

The measured legs back this up — the far leg matches the near leg shifted half
a cycle with correlation **0.936** (thigh) and **0.896** (calf). The arms
correlate at only 0.11, which reflects the unusable far-arm measurement rather
than any asymmetry in the walk.

## The root is solved, not measured

The driver is a prepared Muybridge plate, and its twelve source frames are
separate 1887 exposures with imperfect registration. That jitter moves the
whole frame, so it corrupts any absolute position: the measured pelvis wandered
by 0.139 leg lengths, and the supporting ankle drifted 0.302 leg lengths
vertically across the cycle. A real planted foot does not move at all.

Segment *angles* are immune, because translating an entire frame cannot change
the angle between two points inside it. So the angles are trusted and the root
is recovered from them: the supporting leg spans hip to floor, which pins the
pelvis height, and the planted foot's rearward drift relative to the hip gives
the forward speed.

What comes out is physically ordinary, which is the point:

| quantity | value | expected for a human walk |
|---|---|---|
| stride per cycle | 1.79 legs | 1.6 – 1.9 |
| stance per foot | 0.568 | ≈ 0.6 |
| double support | 0.136 | 0.10 – 0.15 |
| planted-foot drift | 0.012 legs | 0 (≈1 px on screen) |

`strideLengthPerCycle` is what keeps the feet from skating: a runtime that
moves the character `d` pixels must advance the phase by
`d / (strideLengthPerCycle × legLengthOnScreen)`.

## Known limits

- **Four harmonics.** Raising it to six barely moves the knee and ankle
  residuals (7.5° and 8.3°), so what remains is estimator noise, not detail
  being thrown away. The thigh and arms fit to within 1–2°.
- **The bob may be generous.** The derived pelvis rise and fall is 0.099 leg
  lengths where a real walk is nearer 0.05. It is geometrically consistent —
  the foot still plants — but it inherits the knee angle's noise. Worth a look
  once a rig renders it.
- **Two dropped frames.** The estimator found nothing in driver frames 42 and
  45; both were filled by periodic interpolation before fitting.
- **One driver, one character.** These curves describe Muybridge's walker.
  Retargeting is by leg-length ratio only; a companion with very different
  proportions may need its own driver.
